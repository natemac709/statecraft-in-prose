# Case 001: CISA Internal Controls Tradeoff & Price Premium (`B-423156.2`)

- **Docket:** https://www.gao.gov/products/b-423156.2 (RFQ No. `70RCSJ24Q00000072`)
- **Agency:** Department of Homeland Security, Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)
- **Reviewer Verdict:** Denied (Past performance evaluation and best-value tradeoff upheld on May 22, 2025)

## 0. Stated Purpose

> On July 19, 2024, the agency issued the RFQ for the placement of an order under the General Services Administration Multiple Award Schedule, Special Item Number 541219 (Budget and Financial Management Services), for internal control support services for the CISA Office of Chief Financial Officer Internal Control Subdivision.

## 1. The Prompt

> The RFQ, however, further advised vendors that the agency would not issue an award to a vendor “who presents a considerably higher overall price to achieve only slightly better performance capabilities. Consequently, as non-price evaluations become more equal, price may be of greater significance in the trade-off consideration.”

## 2. The Reply

> The protester also argues that the agency failed to properly consider the qualitative differences between the protester’s and awardee’s past performance records.

## 3. The Reviewer's Note

> While Guidehouse Inc. does show both strong institutional knowledge of CISA’s Internal Controls program (and is the incumbent, automatically presenting less transition risk) and provided more references of performing similar work as a prime contractor, I do not believe this is worth an extra $3.1 [million].

## Plain-English Gloss

CISA opened a GSA Schedule task order competition for internal control support, telling vendors it would not pay a steep price premium for only slightly stronger performance. Guidehouse argued its stronger past performance and incumbency outweighed the lower-priced awardee, but the contracting officer concluded that Guidehouse's extra institutional and reference advantages did not justify roughly $3.1 million more, and GAO denied the protest.

- **Later Contract Record:** Protest `B-423156.2` denied May 22, 2025 following reevaluation and award to BDO Public Sector, LLC (`$10,196,570.06` vs. Guidehouse `$13,345,430.25` under RFQ `70RCSJ24Q00000072`).

## Nuance Lost in Translation

A plain-English summary flattens the distinction between *having* an advantage on paper (`"strong institutional knowledge"`, `"incumbent"`, `"more references"`) and that advantage being *worth* a specific dollar/percentage spread (`"$3.1 [million]"` / `"23.60 [percent]"`). In procurement prose, a vendor can win high marks on qualitative comparison while still losing the tradeoff because the solicitation's decision rule sets a threshold on price-versus-capability return.

## Grammar & Structure Notes

- **Tags:** `active_human`, `firm`, `concrete`, `noun_stack`, `quotes_concrete_detail`.
- **Agency & Program Framing:** Heavy bureaucratic noun chains (`"internal control support services"`, `"Budget and Financial Management Services"`) ground a concrete task-order scope.
- **Conditional Rule vs. Decisive Judgment:** The RFQ sets a conditional rule (`"may be of greater significance"`), while the contracting officer states a direct, first-person value judgment (`"I do not believe this is worth an extra $3.1 [million]"`).
- **Concrete Quantifiers:** Specific dollar and percentage figures anchor the tradeoff rationale rather than leaving the price gap vague.
- **Uptake Pattern:** `quotes_concrete_detail` — the reviewer explicitly names the incumbent/institutional advantages and ties the rejection of a higher rating/award directly to concrete price-premium figures.
