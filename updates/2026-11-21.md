# Executive Summary: Cases 001–003

Pre-completion reviews give writers and reviewers a structured way to examine essential claims before submitting or releasing a work product. Cases 001–003 draw on government contracting records to trace three potential argumentation failures tied to governing rules, supporting records, or actual execution. Writers in federal contracting—along with writers drafting technical specifications, policy notes, or operational plans in other fields—can use these findings when evaluating draft prose.

---

## Failure Modes Review

In engineering, a **failure mode** identifies **how** a component, system, or process might fail to perform its intended function under stress or changing conditions. Reviewers can apply failure modes alongside open-ended commentary or style checklists to test drafted prose. Open-ended feedback often centers on surface tone or polish, whereas a failure-mode check pinpoints where a claim struggles under closer inspection—whether a human wrote the draft, an AI tool assisted, or both contributed:

- **Case 001 — Leaning Into Rulesets, Not Familiarity:** Examines claims where broad domain familiarity stands in place of explicit alignment with a stated requirement, credential, threshold, or deadline.
- **Case 002 — Verifying Claims With Proof:** Examines broad summaries, loose figures, or shifting details that lose support when checked against underlying metrics, named owners, or matching facts across sections.
- **Case 003 — Adjacent or Actual Expertise:** Examines statements that cite related work or stated intent rather than naming concrete tools, sequences, controls, and time limits.

## Data Collection and Study

The `data/` directory adds new entries—Cases 001–003—by testing draft claims against verification questions rather than collecting surface edits:

- **Tested against check questions:** Entries remained in the dataset when a statement looked plausible on an initial read yet raised a question under a rule check, a cross-section check, or a look at operational steps.
- **Trimmed for clarity:** Cosmetic wording adjustments and narrow context details were set aside so the underlying structural pattern stayed visible.
- **Grouped into three cases:** Entries were sorted into Cases 001, 002, and 003 to give reviewers distinct lenses for inspecting drafts.

## Findings Exploration and Next Steps

Unverified claims, mismatched figures, and missing operating details erode reader confidence when evaluators, auditors, or careful readers test a document. Analyzing these three cases surfaces clear structural patterns that readers can examine further across the dataset:

- **Alignment with stated gates (Case 001):** Findings show that subject familiarity does not substitute for explicit proof that a draft satisfies a stated rule, threshold, credential, or deadline.
- **Internal consistency across sections (Case 002):** Findings highlight the risk of metric drift, where figures, assigned leads, dates, or definitions conflict across different parts of a single document.
- **Specification of operating mechanics (Case 003):** Findings indicate that citing related experience or intent leaves execution unproven unless the text identifies concrete tools, step sequences, oversight checks, and schedules.
- **Repository exploration:** The `data/` directory provides full records and case-level details for readers who want to inspect how these structural vulnerabilities appear in technical and institutional writing.
